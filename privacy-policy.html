Sphinx

Privacy policy

Last updated: 2026-09-26

Sphinx is a party word-guessing game made by a single independent developer (not a company). This policy explains what information the app handles and why. If anything here is unclear, contact joaorodr84@gmail.com.

Summary

Sphinx works entirely offline. There's no account to create, no login, and no ads. Nothing you do in the game — the words you see, your score, photos you take, how you hold your phone — is uploaded anywhere by Sphinx, with one exception: if the app crashes or hits an error, a technical error report may be sent to a crash-reporting service so the bug can be fixed. That's covered in detail below.

Information Sphinx handles

Score history. Sphinx keeps a local history of rounds you've played (category, score, date) so you can look back at past games. This is stored only on your device, using standard on-device app storage. It is never sent anywhere. You can erase it at any time from the History screen's "Clear history" button (which asks you to confirm before deleting), and it's removed automatically if you uninstall the app.

Camera (optional). After a round, Sphinx can offer to let you take a photo of your group using your device's own camera app. This is entirely optional — you can always tap "Not now." Sphinx shows the photo back to you in a frame with a caption (the app name, the category, your score and the date). If you tap Save, that framed picture is added to your device's photo library, and only then does Sphinx ask for permission to add photos to it — the narrowest kind of permission your device offers, which lets Sphinx add a picture but never look at the ones you already have. Sphinx does not upload the photo, share it, or keep a copy of its own: the temporary files it works from are deleted when you're done with the preview. Once saved, the picture is in your photo library like any other and is yours to keep, share or delete from there. Sphinx only asks for camera access, never microphone access, and only requests each permission the first time you try to use the feature that needs it.

Motion sensors (optional). Sphinx has two optional settings — Forehead mode and Tilt gestures — that use your device's built-in motion sensors (the same kind used for screen rotation) as an alternative way to play, instead of tapping buttons. Both are off by default and only turn on if you enable them in Settings. Motion readings are processed in the moment, on your device, purely to detect a tilt gesture — they are never recorded, stored, or sent anywhere.

Settings and preferences. Choices you make in Settings (theme, language, timer length, and so on) are saved locally on your device so the app remembers them between sessions. They are never transmitted anywhere.

Crash and error reports. If Sphinx crashes or hits an unexpected error, a technical error report (what went wrong, which part of the app, and basic device/app information such as OS version and app version) may be sent to Sentry, a third-party crash-reporting service, to help identify and fix the bug. This is the only network transmission Sphinx performs. These reports are diagnostic — they are not used for advertising, profiling, or tracking you across apps, and Sphinx does not intentionally include your score history, photos, or any text you've typed in an error report. As with most crash-reporting tools, these reports may include your device's IP address as an incidental part of the network request; Sentry's own privacy policy (sentry.io/privacy) governs how it's handled once received. You can read more about Sentry at sentry.io.

What Sphinx does not do

  • No account or login — Sphinx doesn't ask for your name, email, or any other identifying information.
  • No advertising and no ad-tracking SDKs.
  • No analytics or usage-tracking SDKs beyond the crash reporting described above.
  • No selling or sharing of your information with third parties, beyond the crash-reporting service necessary to operate that one feature.

Children's privacy

Sphinx includes an age-band setting (kids / teens / adult) that only changes which words appear in a round — it does not collect anyone's age, identity, or any other personal information, from children or adults. Sphinx does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Data retention and deletion

Score history, settings, and any locally cached data live entirely on your device. You can clear score history at any time from the History screen, or remove everything Sphinx has stored by uninstalling the app. Sphinx has no server-side copy of anything to separately delete, because nothing but crash/error reports (see above) is ever sent off your device.

Changes to this policy

If what Sphinx collects or how it's handled changes, this document will be updated and the "Last updated" date above will change accordingly.
